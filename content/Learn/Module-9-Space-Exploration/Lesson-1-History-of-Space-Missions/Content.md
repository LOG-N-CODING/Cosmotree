# Lesson 1: History of Space Missions

The history of space exploration represents humanity's greatest technological achievement, beginning with the Soviet Union's launch of Sputnik 1 in 1957. This basketball-sized satellite marked the dawn of the Space Age and triggered intense competition between superpowers. The early years saw rapid progress as nations raced to demonstrate technological superiority through increasingly ambitious missions.

Human spaceflight began when Yuri Gagarin orbited Earth in 1961, proving that humans could survive in space. The Apollo program culminated in Neil Armstrong's historic moon landing in 1969, fulfilling President Kennedy's bold promise to reach the lunar surface before the decade's end. These achievements required unprecedented international cooperation and technological innovation.

Robotic exploration has revealed the mysteries of our solar system through missions like Voyager, which provided stunning images of outer planets, and Viking, which first successfully landed on Mars. Modern missions continue this legacy with rovers exploring Martian terrain and spacecraft studying Jupiter's moons. The International Space Station represents the pinnacle of international collaboration, hosting continuous human presence in space for over two decades while conducting vital scientific research in microgravity conditions.
